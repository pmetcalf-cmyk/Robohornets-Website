Outreach & Mentorship

Growing the Next Generation of the Hive

Every RoboHornets member gives back — because our own program started the same way.

🐝

Robot Nation Pipeline

Team 21622 is part of Harrison Community Schools' Robot Nation pipeline — alongside FLL teams #61090 and #63040 and FRC's Stingers of Steel (#6642) — a K–12 robotics program built to grow together.

🔧

STEM Kits & Demos

We give out free STEM kits and run hands-on robot demos at community events like the 4th of July parade and our local street fair.

🤝

Rookie Team Support

We share CAD files, code, and build advice with newer FIRST teams in our district — competing hard on the field, helping freely off it.

Sustainability & Growth

Our Business & Sustainability Plan

A program built to outlast any single roster of students.

$

Diversified Funding

We fund the season through a mix of school district support, local business sponsorships, grants, and team fundraising — no single source carries the whole budget.

📈

Year-Over-Year Growth

Each season we document lessons learned and carry over CAD and code libraries so institutional knowledge doesn't reset to zero. Starting this season, we're introducing formal leadership roles and assigned jobs for every team member.

🎓

Beyond Middle School

Members who age out of RoboHornets often continue into Harrison's FRC team, Stingers of Steel (#6642), as high schoolers — continuing the Robot Nation pipeline.

Culture

Diversity, Equity & Inclusion

A team is only as strong as every voice inside it.

Open Doors

No tryouts, no prerequisite experience — any Harrison student curious about robotics, business, art, or outreach has a role on RoboHornets.

Every Skill Has a Home

Beyond build and code, students lead our design and social media sub-teams — recognizing that competitive robotics needs more than engineers.

Accessible Participation

Fundraising covers member costs so finances are never a barrier to joining, traveling to competitions, or taking on a leadership role.

Representation Matters

We actively recruit across Harrison Community Schools as a whole — including homeschooled students and incoming 6th graders from the elementary school — to reach students who might not otherwise consider STEM.
