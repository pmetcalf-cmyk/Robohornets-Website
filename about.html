About Us

Our Story & Mission

RoboHornets brings together students who love building things, solving problems, and competing as a team.

Our Mission

Engineering Confidence, One Season at a Time

RoboHornets exists to give Harrison students hands-on experience with real engineering, coding, and design challenges — the same skills used by professional engineers — while building the teamwork and confidence that come from competing together.

Every season starts with a new game challenge. Students form sub-teams for mechanical design, programming, strategy, and outreach, then spend weeks building a robot from the ground up before taking it to competition.

What We Believe

We compete under FIRST's core value of Gracious Professionalism — fierce competition on the field, genuine support off it.

  • Students design and build — mentors guide, not do
  • Every member has a role, from CAD to outreach
  • We help other teams succeed, even our opponents
  • Failure is part of the design process
FIRST Core Values

What Guides Every Decision We Make

RoboHornets holds itself to FIRST's six Core Values — on the field, in the shop, and in the community. See how we put them into practice on our Impact page.

Discovery

We explore new skills and ideas beyond what any single student already knows.

Innovation

We use creative thinking and persistence to solve problems, not just off-the-shelf answers.

Impact

We apply what we learn to improve our team, our school, and our community.

Inclusion

We respect each other and embrace differences of skill, background, and opinion.

Teamwork

We are stronger when we work together — Coopertition over pure competition.

Fun

We celebrate what we do, because enjoying the work is what keeps the hive coming back.

Program History

How the Hive Grew

From a first-year rookie squad to a returning competitive presence in FIRST Tech Challenge.

1

Rookie Season

Team 21622 formed at Harrison Schools and fielded its first competition robot, learning the FTC season cycle from kickoff to competition.

2

Building Momentum

We grew our roster, formalized sub-teams for build, code, and outreach, and started documenting our design process in a real engineering notebook.

3

Today

RoboHornets competes each season with a full student-run build, an active outreach program, and a growing sponsor network behind it.
